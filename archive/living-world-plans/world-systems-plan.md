# Living World — world systems plan

> **Archived 2026-09-27 — historical material, not current rules.** Setting references describe a removed setting. Use the repository’s `rules/` and `modules/` indexes for current material.

**Status:** design proposal, not playable rules · **Revised:** 2026-09-24  
**Owns:** persistent projects, clocks, resources, advancement, and the expansion backlog.

## 1. The engine of play

**Need or ambition → player invitation → scene → recorded result → DM world response → new situation.**

A scene need not earn a reward or move a clock to matter. Relationships and character development are sufficient reasons to play. Players can suggest any plausible initiative; published opportunities help people find partners and never gate access to play.

At launch prepare two connected neighbourhoods, three recurring public locations, six NPC contacts, and six open needs. These are content budgets, not new Port Vane canon. Give each place something to protect, something scarce, and an institutional constraint. Track only conditions that currently affect choices.

## 2. Player projects and collective action

Propose a shared project record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Objective | A concrete change and who benefits. |
| Sponsor and contributors | One contact; any willing PCs may contribute. |
| Scope | What PCs can attempt themselves; what world response needs a DM. |
| Milestones | Usually 3–5 different obstacles, with evidence required for each. |
| Inputs | Time, access, materials, expertise, promises; DM quotes mechanical costs first. |
| Opposition and risk | Who might resist, what can go wrong, and what happens if abandoned. |
| Completion | A bounded asset, permission, local improvement, or discovery; no unspecified permanent bonus. |

Players can organise immediately. Milestone completion and shared benefits wait for DM ratification. Repeating the same conversation or dividing it across more threads produces no extra progress. Failed attempts may change the obstacle instead of erasing all prior work.

**Starting capacity proposal:** one sponsored project per PC; contributing elsewhere is allowed, subject to chronology and committed resources. Staff scope up to six active world-changing projects server-wide at first. Extra ideas remain recruitment/planning scenes without promised implementation dates.

**Example:** restoring a Flats pump needs access, serviceable parts, and a safe installation. Negotiating permission, arranging a supply exchange, and protecting the workers are different contributions. The DM confirms each obstacle is overcome and records the resulting neighbourhood condition. No required combat, scene count, or single prescribed solution.

## 3. Clocks and world developments

Use a clock only for a threat or undertaking that requires several meaningful changes. Record its name, current state, trigger, available counterplay, owner, visibility, and completion consequence. Start with at most three public world clocks; hide causes only when players have discoverable clues.

**Prototype:** four or six segments. A qualifying adjudicated event changes one segment unless its published rule says otherwise. Describe what that movement means in the fiction. A weekly digest reports movement; it does not automatically advance every clock. A fictional deadline advances only when the DM establishes elapsed fictional time.

This borrows the general practice of tracking obstacles and approaching danger from [Blades in the Dark's progress clocks](https://bladesinthedark.com/progress-clocks). The proposed VBG triggers, authority, and rewards are separate design work, not imported Blades rules.

At each world update, the assigned DM reviews recent results, advances only supported developments, publishes a short situation report, and offers at least two possible avenues of response. Unchosen problems can develop; an absent player's debt, injury, or property does not deteriorate merely because they missed real-world posts.

### Domain loudness and Carrion Attention

This section owns the proposed persistent loudness procedure; combat reports events to it. Port Vane supplies the fictional connection between Domain use and Carrion.

- Each approved Domain effect needs a loudness entry describing reach and attraction. Quiet does not mean invisible or always safe.
- Prototype a **local Carrion Attention clock** tied to a named area and current incident. It is distinct from a city's slower incursion development; the same event is not charged to both without a disclosed link.
- A candidate effect card quotes 0, 1, or 2 attention segments before use. Zero applies only where the approved quiet effect attracts nothing at that distance. Final values depend on P1 and combat testing.
- Every event has a scene/cycle/effect ID. The DM or authorised encounter runner records it once, including sustained use, overlapping scenes, existing Carrion, and attracted creatures. Runners apply only packet-defined attention responses; wider effects go to staff. Creating a new thread never resets attention.
- On a four-segment trial clock, two segments reveal a warning and counterplay; four trigger the encounter's announced response, such as an approaching patrol of Shiverers. Define what happens after that response rather than automatically spawning another wave every cycle.
- Specify dispersal, concealment, and decay on the incident card. Closing a scene, sleeping in real time, or using a stabiliser does not automatically erase attention.

This is a prototype specification, not a currently usable clock. Test whether loud effects solve sufficiently difficult problems to justify the risk, whether quiet powers still matter, and whether Domainless PCs have effective intervention options.

### Burnout and stabilisers

Burnout has no settled mechanical procedure in VBG 2.2. Define it before selling a mechanical cure. Keep **personal overuse** separate from **external Carrion Attention**: medicine may manage the former but cannot un-summon predators.

Recommended first experiment: an explicitly declared overchannel option can incur a named overuse condition; stabilisers prevent or treat that condition under a written item rule. Ordinary Strain still pays for abilities and harm, and ordinary full rest still restores it. Overchannel benefits, triggers, severity, treatment, and death interactions require their own local rules before play.

Compare this against a modest cumulative exposure model during testing. Do not assume overchannel-only dependence will support the setting's pervasive drug debt. Resolve that tension in the Port Vane adapter before general launch; avoid silently removing natural recovery or charging drugs every real-world day.

## 4. Factions, crews, and standing

| System | Launch proposal | Later development |
|---|---|---|
| Faction standing | DM-authored relationship note: known deeds, obligations, access, current dispute | Approve separate standing tracks under P2 only if notes prove insufficient. |
| Membership and rank | Use setting membership constraints; advancement needs faction-appropriate evidence, prerequisites, costs, and a DM decision | Mechanical rank benefits require P4. Cult ranks reflect transformation rather than appointment and need approved transformation rules. |
| Divided loyalty | Present incompatible obligations openly to the player; record their choice and separately adjudicate each response | Exposure procedure and consequence menu after P3. |
| PC crews | Shared purpose, roster, home location, access rules, and asset record | Workshops, networks, specialised collective projects. |
| Local influence | Specific permissions or relationships | A larger role in district decisions without replacing setting institutions. |

A crew is a social organisation, not a fourth faction or a shortcut around membership caps. A faction office gives no out-of-character authority over another player. A favour buys only what its owner freely agrees to provide.

## 5. Economy, gear, and debt

Start with a closed, small catalog and **abstract payout bands**, not a simulated city market. P5 needs a conversion table from each band to defined purchasing power; “large bounty” alone cannot buy anything. D2 must supply the items, quantities, and slots.

Design these together before economic play:

| Component | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Starting provision | Up to two approved items under the baseline; explicitly list any local changes. |
| Income | Published contract awards or salvage claims; declare division before work. Project grants are restricted funds, not personal income. |
| Access | Who can sell, loan, requisition, or supply an item, including the first route to Armor (Q4). |
| Spending | Stabiliser treatment, medicine, repairs, ammunition if used, project inputs, and improved access. |
| Debt | Named creditor, finite obligation, repayment terms, trigger, and capped consequence. |
| Asset ownership | Owner, custodians, location, usage rights, transfer history, and outstanding reservations. |

**Proposed interpretation of Port Vane's permanent debt:** individual obligations can be paid or renegotiated while recurring fictional needs sustain Ledger dependence. Record specific relief when players earn it; never use unexplained interest to cancel an achievement. Whether a Ledger operator can become wholly debt-free remains a setting decision. Allowing that would explicitly depart from Factions §2 and must enter the adopted override register.

A project proposal issues no money. A DM-approved grant names the project-held account, authorised purchases, any separately approved wages, and where unused funds return. Abandoning a project cannot convert its budget into personal purchasing power; transfers follow the ordinary transaction record.

**Transaction workflow:** PCs agree terms → submit one exchange record → DM confirms ownership, availability, slots, and any access restriction → atomically debit and credit the affected records. Promised goods cannot be used before confirmation. Staff quote routine catalog transactions in advance and batch the final approvals; players do not grant themselves stock or loot. The [Encounter Running extension](encounter-running-plan.md) separately permits endorsed, uninvolved reviewers to validate fixed preauthorised packet rewards; general transactions still require a DM.

Frequent monster fights reserve an approved instance and reward allowance before opening. Reusable packets never imply endless local monsters, salvage, or payouts. Immediate combat expenditure, ordinary packet-defined recovery, and current harm are recorded during play; new rewards remain unavailable until independent validation. A clean resource snapshot supports the next fight without waiting for its previous payout. Repeated kills, rotating hosts, linked waves, and retreat/re-entry cannot duplicate a claim.

Prevent obvious loops: one event pays once; one corpse produces one registered salvage claim; an item cannot be sold or reserved twice; paper-only self-trades create no income; staff do not approve their own character's benefits. Distinguish possessions in storage from items actually carried. Housing does not enlarge the 4–6-slot carrying limit.

Launch crafting as a few DM-approved repair or project recipes with explicit inputs and outputs. Defer freeform manufacturing, player-run shops, speculation, and passive income until the resource budget has survived testing. Economic play must support medics, organisers, and investigators as well as monster hunters.

## 6. Progression without activity grinding

Keep the baseline PP purchase costs, caps, and relevant-trait limits. This section proposes a local answer to D3's missing award amounts; it does not change the core log.

**Test two-week review periods:** award 2 PP for one evidenced meaningful contribution, plus 1 PP for an additional distinct contribution, maximum 3 PP per player across their characters. Contributions may be social, practical, investigative, or martial. Award to the participating character; no duplicated alt claims. These periods organise reviews, not when players may play.

- Judge changed commitments, consequential decisions, overcoming an obstacle, or substantial help; never prose length, post count, dice count, attendance streaks, or popularity votes.
- A quiet relationship scene may qualify when it changes a commitment; nobody must disclose private scene content publicly. A reviewing DM needs an agreed summary.
- Failure can qualify when the attempt meaningfully develops play. Deliberately repeating the same failure cannot.
- Equal contributions receive equal awards regardless of faction or combat access. No kill bounties in PP and no separate combat advancement pool.
- A play-and-run PC is eligible under the same contribution cap as other PCs, with independent review. Running earns no extra mechanical reward, and runner-only work does not advance an off-scene character. Frequent combat creates more opportunities for play, not a bypass around the cap.
- Record event IDs and evidence; a result claimed in multiple periods is awarded once.
- Absence loses no existing PP. Returning/new characters get useful contacts and access to appropriately scoped situations; test numerical catch-up only after progression data shows a gap.

At 2–3 PP per two weeks, a 5 PP Minor Trait takes roughly 4–6 weeks of qualifying play, and a 15 PP Stat increase takes roughly 10–16 weeks. Test that pace with players before adoption. If it is too slow, change one documented award schedule, not ad hoc bonuses to frequent posters.

## 7. Additional modules worth adding

| Addition | Play it generates | Priority / dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Relationships and obligations | Recurring care, rivalry, promises, and difficult choices | Launch; [roleplay module](player-roleplay-module-plan.md). |
| Rumours and local journalism | Witness interviews, information exchanges, public accountability | Launch; identify established facts, PC claims, and attributed rumours. DM owns discoveries. |
| Clinics and mutual aid | Treatment, medicine negotiations, protecting vulnerable neighbours | Pilot; D1/D2 and treatment rules. |
| Housing and shared places | Recurring social scenes, hospitality, contested upkeep | Pilot; permissions and asset ledger. No unlimited inventory or passive income. |
| Investigation cases | Several groups contribute evidence from different scenes | Pilot; DM case sheet with independent clue routes and an evidence record. |
| Professions and workshops | Noncombat expertise, commissions, useful interdependence | Expansion; tested items, crafting costs, and capped outputs. |
| Civic proceedings | Hearings, testimony, residents' demands, resource negotiations | Expansion; staff adjudicate institutions; no compulsory PC belief changes. |
| Festivals, memorials, public competitions | Low-pressure entry points and relationships across groups | Pilot; reward participation's consequences, not event attendance. |
| District crises | Linked evacuation, logistics, diplomacy, investigation, and combat scenes | Expansion; each group has an independent objective and one shared event ID. |
| Travel and distant communities | Supply lines, contact networks, rescue, migration | Later; D5 transition rules and Port Vane's Bleed-Belt. |
| Legacy and retirement | Characters leave institutions, successors, and remembered changes | Later; no automatic transfer of power or wealth to an alternate character. |

Give each new subsystem an owner, entry procedure, cost, outcome record, and limit on staff work before adding it. A decorative spreadsheet that generates no decisions should be removed.
