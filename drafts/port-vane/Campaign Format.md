# Port Vane — Campaign Format

**Status:** working draft · **Revised:** 2026-09-24
**Owns:** how this campaign is structured, who decides what, and the membership rules.

> Mechanics come from [VBG 2.2](../vbg-2.2/README.md). This document governs structure, not resolution.

## 1. Shape of the campaign

- **Player-centric.** Not West Marches. No required expedition-and-return loop, no fixed quest window, no quest board gating play.
- **Factions hold power.** Players do not displace or replace them. Play happens inside, between, and against them.
- **All four bodies are playable, and none is the antagonist.** Iron Ledger (Contract and Salvage), District Marshals, Rupture Cult — and remaining a Stray is a complete path, not a waiting room. The **Carrion** are the standing threat. See [Factions](Factions.md) §1.
- **Everyone starts a Stray.** Unaffiliated is the default state, not a faction. Affiliation is acquired and costs something.
- **Two memberships maximum.** See §3.
- **Roleplay runs two ways.** Players may run scenes themselves or with a DM present. Authority split in §2.
- **The city was already running.** See §6.

## 2. Who decides what

The governing principle:

> **Players decide what their characters do. Only a DM decides what the world does back.**

| Self-run scenes may decide | Requires a DM |
|---|---|
| Dialogue, argument, persuasion between PCs | Faction standing changes |
| Relationships, promises, grudges, alliances | Character death or permanent injury |
| Positioning and in-fiction time | Gear gained or lost |
| Minor resource use | New information about the world |
| Information passed between players | Anything inflicted on another PC without consent |
| What a character believes, intends, or conceals | Faction response to player action |

### Ratification

Self-run scenes are **logged and ratified in batch**, not approved live. A DM reads the log afterward and confirms or adjusts consequences.

This keeps DM load low without surrendering authority, and it closes the obvious exploit: players cannot farm standing in scenes they adjudicate themselves, because standing was never theirs to grant.

A scene log should record: who was present, what was decided, what was claimed, and anything the players believe should change as a result.

## 3. Memberships

A character may hold **at most two** memberships. Three-way loyalty produces paralysis rather than drama.

**No pairing is forbidden.** Every combination is playable; what differs is what it costs to keep and what happens when it surfaces.

| Pair | Status | What it means in play |
|---|---|---|
| Ledger Contract ↔ Ledger Salvage | **Open** | Same institution, different division. Nobody hides it. The friction is internal and constant. |
| Ledger ↔ Marshals | **Concealed** | A badge on the corporate payroll, or a contractor feeding the precinct. Both sides would call it a conflict of interest; neither would call it unthinkable. |
| Ledger ↔ Cult | **Dangerous** | The sharpest pair in the city. A Cleaver quietly supplying the Hollowed, or a Tethered holding a licence to stay near the stabiliser supply. Exposure ends a career on one side and a life on the other. |
| Marshals ↔ Cult | **Dangerous** | An officer whose brother is Tethered, and who has stopped pretending it is only family. Exposure costs the badge, and probably the precinct's trust in every officer who knew. |
| Any ↔ Stray | **Not a pair** | Stray is the absence of membership, not a membership. |

**Concealed** means playable while hidden, with ordinary consequences on exposure. **Dangerous** means the same, with severe ones: if it surfaces, the side with less to lose acts first, and acts hard. Concealment is a fiction problem, not a roll — nobody rolls to keep a secret; people keep secrets by how they behave in scenes.

A DM adjudicates exposure, as with everything the world does back (§2).

### Conflict of interest

When both memberships issue opposed demands in the same span of play, the character does not get to satisfy both. They choose, and the one they did not satisfy notices.

The character may:

- **Comply with one, openly.** The other faction records it. Standing with them falls.
- **Comply with one, and conceal it.** Nothing falls yet. The concealment becomes a live thing a DM may bring back.
- **Refuse both.** Both record it. Refusing is often the cheapest option and always the loneliest.

No roll resolves this. It is a decision, and the DM plays out what each body does with it.

### Internal loyalty

Because the Ledger is a vertical monopoly, divided-loyalty stories also happen **inside** it: Contract against Salvage, one Director against another, a Line Cleaver quietly feeding a rival Board Partner. With the Cult now playable and every pairing legal, this is one option among several rather than the only source of the theme — which resolves [P7](Open%20Questions.md).

### Leaving

A character may renounce or lose every membership and return to being a Stray. Nobody in Port Vane leaves cleanly. The Ledger revokes the Sanction and stops refining for you; the Marshals keep the file; the Cult keeps visiting.

## 4. Scenes

A scene is any contained situation with shared stakes — VBG owns what happens mechanically inside one.

| Scene type | Typically run |
|---|---|
| Incursion, contract, open violence | With a DM |
| Negotiation, faction politics, interrogation | Either |
| Downtime, relationships, planning, resupply | Self-run |

Threat scenes use the VBG resolution cycle. **Non-threat play is deliberately freeform** — ordinary resolution, no cycle, no action economy ([Player Rulebook](../vbg-2.2/Player%20Rulebook.md) §4). That is what makes self-run scenes workable: there is no procedure a DM has to be present to run.

Long travel is the exception and uses a **Transition Scene**. The [Bleed-Belt](Setting%20Guide.md) §5 is built out of them.

Scene conditions keep running underneath all of it. A self-run scene in a static corridor is still a scene with no working phone in it.

## 5. Standing

Faction standing is **pending** — how it is tracked, what moves it, and whether gaining with one faction costs another are unresolved. See [P2](Open%20Questions.md).

Until settled, treat standing as fiction the DM narrates rather than a number anyone tracks.

## 6. The world was already running

Port Vane is not waiting for the players, and it was not built the moment they arrived. Before a single character posts, the city has ongoing business of its own — and it advances whether or not anyone interacts with it.

This is the campaign's default posture, and it changes how a DM prepares:

- **Start clocks before session one.** Each faction should have two or three things it is currently trying to do, with a visible state, written down before any player sees them. A stabiliser shipment is late. A precinct is being quietly defunded. A Cairn in the Sinks is four weeks old and nobody has reported it.
- **Advance them on a schedule, not on attention.** A clock moves because time passed. If the players never touch the late shipment, it does not freeze — the shortage reaches the outer blocks, bootleg prices double, and three Strays the players know start looking desperate.
- **Consequences existed before the players did.** A ruined block has a reason, a name, and somebody still angry about it. Players should be able to find out why things are the way they are, and the answer should not be "so that this scene works."
- **Players change the world by intersecting it.** They do not start it. A player who ignores every faction clock still lives in a city being shaped by them, which is most of what makes the setting feel inhabited.
- **Write what happens without them.** When a DM prepares a week, they should be able to state what would have happened had no player posted. Play is the difference between that and what actually happened.

The practical effect: a DM never has to invent a reason for something to be going on, and players never arrive at a place that was idling until they showed up.
