# Port Vane — Carrion

**Status:** working draft · **Revised:** 2026-09-24
**Owns:** creature types, biology, and behaviour.

> Creature construction and all stat values belong to the [VBG Creature Blueprint](../vbg-2.2/Creature%20Blueprint.md).

## 1. What they are

**The Carrion are the campaign's antagonist, and the only one.** No faction in Port Vane fills that role ([Factions](Factions.md) §1). When a scene needs something that cannot be reasoned with, bought off, or understood as having a point of view, it comes out of a tear.

They are not an invading army. No generals, no diplomacy, no ideology. Metaphysical apex parasites running on three instincts. That is what makes them usable as the standing threat in a setting where every human party is sympathetic: there is nothing to negotiate with, and no version of the story where they were misunderstood.

| Instinct | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Hunger for biomass** | Their cells decay under Earth atmospheric physics. To avoid dissolving they must constantly consume organic matter and stitch it into their own anatomy. |
| **Addiction to Stigma** | Active Domain use is a beacon. A fleeing civilian is food; a Cleaver igniting an ability is irresistible. Consuming a Domain user temporarily grants fragments of that power. **Mechanically this is a card field, not a player cost** — see [Domains](Domains.md) §2. |
| **Hiving** | A Spawn left alive for days anchors to infrastructure — subway tunnel, ventilation shaft, condemned factory — fusing flesh with rebar and wiring into a **Cairn**, which opens permanent secondary tears. |

The second instinct is the setting's central tactical fact, and it cuts both ways. Using your Domain calls the worst thing in the district toward you — which is a disaster when you did not mean to, and the single most useful thing a Domain user can do when they did. Pulling a Carapace-Render off a crowd is a job, and only one person on the crew can do it.

## 2. Tiers

| Tier | Name | Street name | Proposed VBG tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Shiverers | Gutter-Ticks | Minor — 1 Resistance |
| **II** | Carapace-Renders | Meat-Engines | Standard to Major — 2–4 |
| **III** | Cairn-Titans | Architects, City-Blights | Boss — 5+ |
| **IV** | Unclassified | The Cold Silents, Hollow-Born | Not for early play — see §2.4 |

### Tier I — Shiverers

**Biology.** Dog- to human-sized masses of translucent gelatinous tissue, desperately stitching together whatever organic refuse is nearby — dead pigeons, rats, rotting strays, human garbage. Their cellular bonds are rejected by Earth physics, so they weep pale lymphatic fluid and twitch constantly. Rarely last forty-eight hours outside a Rupture before liquefying into toxic sludge.

**Senses.** Smell and ground vibration. Effectively blind, and indifferent to light or darkness — a lamp neither helps nor betrays you against them, and Hush does nothing.

**Behaviour.** Cowardly opportunistic pack predators. Nest in flooded storm drains, abandoned track, air ducts. Avoid well-lit thoroughfares. Too stupid to bypass a barricade, but a swarm of six to a dozen can drag down an unpowered officer or a distracted hunter.

**Elimination.** Routine — one hit each, as with anything at Minor tier. Buckshot, a focused kinetic burst, or pressurised lye ruptures their fluid cavities. The danger is never one Shiverer; it is that there are nine and you have one Major Action per cycle.

**Salvage.** Bottom-barrel, and the reason Tier I is worth clearing at all: sludge is vacuumed up and refined into ordinary stabilisers. A nest is a week of Strain for somebody. Eat one raw and it is worth nothing to anyone afterwards.

### Tier II — Carapace-Renders

**Biology.** Enraged silverback up to delivery-van sized. Achieved temporary equilibrium by fusing organic matter with inorganic urban material — rebar, asphalt, engine blocks, chain-link grafted into calcified plate. Bleed thick tar smelling of burning sulfur.

**Behaviour.** Aggressive, territorial, biologically addicted to Stigma. Largely ignores unpowered civilians while an active Domain user is nearby. When an ability ignites, it frenzies and charges through brick walls to reach the user.

**Senses.** Ordinary sight and hearing, overridden entirely by Domain use: an ability inside its district reads to it as a flare on an empty horizon. It will abandon a civilian mid-kill to reach one.

**Adaptive consumption.** If it consumes a Domain user it assimilates fragments of that ability for several hours — swallow a pyromancer and its jaw ignites into white-hot slag.

**Elimination.** High risk. The carapace is a **creature condition**, not extra Resistance: until it is cracked, ordinary attacks remove no boxes at all. Breaching weapons, heavy ordnance, Rust, Solvent, or a fall onto something sharp get through it; a sidearm does not, however many times it is fired. Once the plate is open, two to four hits finish it like anything else.

**Salvage.** The primary currency of the Exchange floor. Adrenal glands, weaponisable bone spurs, and pure spinal marrow become pharmaceutical-grade stabilisers and anti-anomaly munitions.

### Tier III — Cairn-Titans

**Biology.** Megastructure scale — three storeys to an entire city block. Does not merely hunt; roots into foundational infrastructure, weaving steel, concrete, high-voltage wiring, subway tunnel, and hundreds of corpses into a pulsing bio-mechanical lung.

**Senses.** It does not hunt; it occupies. Anything inside its footprint is detected, and cover and darkness are irrelevant at that scale. Getting out of the footprint is the only answer.

**Dead Static field.** Cripples technology within roughly 800 yards. **Mechanically:** the DM declares Dead Static for the whole engagement, for as long as the Titan is present ([Domains](Domains.md) §2). It never lifts while the fight is on. Phones brick, car batteries rupture, radio screams feedback, CRTs ignite into electrical fires.

**Behaviour.** A siege, not a fight. Acts as mobile incubator and tactical anchor, continuously spawning Tier I from pustules along its flanks while firing barrages of toxic bile and crushed masonry.

**Elimination.** Catastrophic engagements. Marshals evacuate entire zip codes. A Cairn-Titan's Resistance cannot be removed at all until the primary **Heart-Cairn** is exposed — a demolition problem, not a combat one. Killing one requires pooled Contract crews, high-yield ordnance, demolition rigs, and Domain strikes into the opened core. Civilian casualties and ruined infrastructure are effectively guaranteed.

**Lethal by declaration.** A Cairn-Titan is one of the few things in Port Vane whose card states that its actions kill rather than Down a character ([VBG Player Rulebook](../vbg-2.2/Player%20Rulebook.md) §6). A DM running one announces that before the cycle it applies. Nobody should be surprised by it.

**Salvage.** Millions. The core yields calcified metaphysical "pearls" — raw anomalous catalysts that pharmaceutical giants and black-market labs fight covert wars over.

### Tier IV — The Cold Silents

> **Not early-campaign material.** This section is for the DM. Tier IV is a slow background presence, not a plot the players are expected to uncover — see §2.4.

**The anomaly.** Everything known about Carrion biology says they are feral, unstable, rot fast, and generate Dead Static. Tier IV violates every rule.

**Biology.** No visible mutation, no grafted scrap, no cellular decay. To the eye, entirely human. Normal body temperature, ordinary crimson blood, no radio interference — instead the opposite: an eerie deadening of ambient sound within a room.

**Behaviour.** Does not hunger for biomass or rush toward Domain flares. Observes, converses, calculates. Understands currency, corporate hierarchy, and human psychology better than the people running the city.

**The threat.** No Ledger bounty listing exists, because Ledger leadership refuses to admit they do. Whispers among dying Strays suggest several already walk the upper rings — sitting on boards, funding the firms that synthesise stabilisers, timing Ruptures. Not destroying the city. Domesticating it, into a managed pen for harvesting licensed Stigma at scale.

#### 2.4 How to actually use them

The ruling: **Tier IV is not revealed, and not soon.** They are part of why the world is the shape it is, and the campaign works whether or not a single player ever learns they exist.

- **They are background, not a metaplot.** No clock counts down to their reveal. Nothing in the campaign is gated behind noticing them.
- **They produce texture, not clues.** A room where the sound goes flat. A board decision that makes no commercial sense. A Rupture that opened at a convenient hour. These read as ordinary Port Vane strangeness, because they are.
- **The DM writes their effects into faction clocks, not into a mystery.** If a stabiliser firm is being steered, that shows up as the firm's behaviour ([Campaign Format](Campaign%20Format.md) §6). Players interact with the firm.
- **If players do start pulling the thread, let them** — and let it be slow, deniable, and very difficult to prove to anyone who matters. The Ledger's institutional denial is not a cover-up. It is genuine disbelief, which is harder to overcome.
- **They are still Carrion.** Whatever they can discuss, they are not a faction and there is no accommodation with them. That is the line between them and everyone else in this city.

A campaign that never touches Tier IV has lost nothing. That is the point of running it this way.

## 3. Building an encounter

Build cards with the [VBG Creature Blueprint](../vbg-2.2/Creature%20Blueprint.md), which owns the card format and every field it requires.

Port Vane specifics to carry onto the card:

- **Senses.** Required on every card ([VBG Creature Blueprint](../vbg-2.2/Creature%20Blueprint.md) §3), and it carries most of a Carrion's character. Shiverers hunt by smell and vibration and are effectively blind. Carapace-Renders orient on Domain use above everything else. Cairn-Titans do not need senses at the scale they operate on. Write what the creature **cannot** perceive, because that is what a player builds a plan out of.
- **Stigma draw.** For Tier II and above, write the creature's reaction to player Domain use into a card field rather than improvising it. This is the setting's signature and players should be able to see it coming.
- **Noise.** State what the creature itself ticks onto the clock. A Shiverer pack is Quiet; a Carapace-Render coming through a wall is Loud, which is how one incursion becomes three.
- **Discoverable clues.** Every condition needs one. The Ledger's institutional denial means players often learn a creature's rules from Strays or Marshals rather than official briefings.
- **Salvage line.** Note what the corpse is worth and who wants it. The body is usually the reason anyone came.
- **Dead Static radius** for Tier III, since it dictates what technology works in the scene.

**Write danger into conditions, not into Resistance.** VBG has settled that every hit deals exactly 1 damage, permanently ([VBG Player Rulebook](../vbg-2.2/Player%20Rulebook.md) §6). Resistance is therefore how many cycles a creature survives, not how frightening it is. A Carapace-Render is not dangerous because it has 4 boxes — it is dangerous because the plate is a creature condition that stops boxes being removed at all, because it ignores civilians to charge whoever last used a Domain, and because it goes through the wall to do it. Put the tier's identity in its conditions, its threats, and its Stigma draw. Resistance just sets the length of the fight.

## 4. Bounties

Source material quotes dollars: roughly $150–300 for a Tier I, $25,000–80,000 for a Tier II, millions for a Tier III. Those figures are **fiction** — they tell you relative worth, and characters quote them at each other, but nothing in VBG converts them.

What a bounty actually buys is expressed in the [VBG Item Glossary](../vbg-2.2/Item%20Glossary.md) §2 value bands, which is the currency this game has.

| Kill | Bounty band | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier I**, a handful | Petty | Common items, a few material units, **one stabiliser**. |
| **Tier I**, a nest cleared, or **Tier II**, shared | Standing | One **Restricted** item, or **three stabilisers**, or a Sanction renewed without anyone asking questions. |
| **Tier II**, solo or lead share | Substantial | Several Restricted items, or a **Rare** one at a bad rate, or a full resupply for a crew. |
| **Tier III**, any share at all | Career | A **Rare** item outright — a red stabiliser, a suppressed weapon — or a rank, and the attention that comes with having been there. |

Four rules keep this honest:

- **Salvage is where the value is, not the kill.** A corpse the Marshals cordon as evidence, or one that dissolved before the trucks arrived, pays nothing. Getting the body out is usually the actual scene, and it needs a salvage kit ([Domains](Domains.md) §6).
- **A band is not money and does not make change.** A character cannot bank two Standing bounties into a Career one. Bands describe what a payment reaches, and Rare things are obtained in play, never simply bought.
- **Anything eaten is not sold.** A character who consumes raw tissue to get Strain back drops the corpse one band ([Domains](Domains.md) §5). That is the real trade in this economy: the body refills you now, or it refills you later and better after somebody refines it.
- **A bounty is what keeps a Sanction current.** For a licensed Cleaver, the payment matters less than being on the crew list at all ([Domains](Domains.md) §4).

**Why this is the engine.** Stabilisers are the only manufactured source of Strain in Port Vane, and every one of them was cut out of something that came through a tear. That is the whole economy, stated once: you hunt Carrion to get the thing that lets you hunt Carrion, and the Iron Ledger refines all of it.
