# Port Vane — Factions

**Status:** working draft · **Revised:** 2026-09-24
**Owns:** faction composition, aims, tensions, and ranks.

> Membership rules live in [Campaign Format](Campaign%20Format.md) §3. Mechanics come from [VBG 2.2](../vbg-2.2/README.md).

## 1. No faction is the antagonist

Three bodies hold power in Port Vane, and **every one of them is playable**. So is belonging to none of them.

| Body | What it wants | Why a reasonable person joins |
|---|---|---|
| **The Iron Ledger** | To keep the corpse trade running, because it is the only thing standing between this city and an unmanaged incursion. | It is the only organisation that actually kills Carrion at scale, and it pays. |
| **The District Marshals** | Civilians out, alive, at no profit to anyone. | Somebody has to, and nobody else is going to. |
| **The Rupture Cult** | The stabiliser monopoly broken, because a leash is still a leash however well it is administered. | They are not wrong about the leash. |
| **Strays** | To be left alone and stay alive. | Everything above has a price attached. |

**The Carrion are the campaign's threat, and the only one** ([Carrion](Carrion.md) §1). They cannot be negotiated with, bought off, or read sympathetically, and that is precisely what frees every human body in this city to be a place a player might reasonably stand.

This is a deliberate reversal of an earlier draft that named the Rupture Cult as the primary antagonist. The Cult is now written as a faction with a coherent argument, run by people who reached it honestly. A DM running them as mindless villains is running a different setting.

What replaces a villain is **friction**: four bodies with incompatible aims, none of which can be removed, all of which the players must live among.

## 2. Strays — the unaffiliated

**Not a faction.** *Stray* is what Port Vane calls anyone who belongs to nothing: unregistered Domain users, defaulted debtors, tenement vigilantes, people who simply never signed. No leadership, no dues, no collective aim. What they share is exposure.

Every character starts a Stray. Affiliation is acquired, and it costs.

Strays are the contested population — the pool every faction draws from. The Ledger recruits them or hunts them, the Cult preys on them, the Marshals quietly tolerate them because Strays are the only protection the outer blocks have.

### Remaining a Stray is a complete path

It is not a waiting room, and a character who never joins anything has not failed to engage with the campaign.

| What you give up | What you get |
|---|---|
| A supply line. Stabilisers are grey-market or nothing. | No obligations, and no opposed demands to be caught between. |
| Institutional backing when something goes wrong. | Access to every room. Nobody's enemy by default, which the affiliated never are. |
| A rank ladder and the standing that comes with it. | The only position in the city from which all three bodies can be told the truth. |

A Stray campaign runs on favours, blocks, and people rather than contracts and clocks. It is harder to resupply and easier to move.

### Informal standing

Reputation among the unaffiliated, not a rank ladder. Nobody confers it.

| Standing | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bleeder** (Green-Marks) | Newly awakened, freshly defaulted, or recently run. Disorganised and prone to burnout. Kept at arm's length — unmonitored Domain use attracts Carrion and attention. |
| **Warden** (Holdout) | Has lasted long enough on bootleg stabilisers to be trusted. Protects specific blocks, orphanages, squats. The backbone of the underground. |
| **Ghost** (Old Blood) | Years off-grid without mutating, dying, or being taken. Coordinates safehouses and brokers bulk stabiliser shipments. Names fiercely guarded. |

These are reputations, not ranks. Nobody awards them and nothing tracks them.

## 3. The Iron Ledger — "The Exchange"

The centralised monopoly on sanctioned violence and the corpse trade. It takes the contracts, kills the monsters, harvests the parts, refines and sells them, and runs down unregistered users. Vertically integrated: nothing about a dead Carrion escapes it.

**Public pitch:** an emergency liquidation and threat-neutralisation clearinghouse.
**Reality:** a blood-slick exchange where districts trade bounties and operators bid against each other. If you have a registered Domain, you either work the floor or become a listing on it.

**The sympathetic reading, which a DM should take seriously:** the Ledger is the only body in Port Vane that reliably kills Tier II and above. Every person in this city who did not die in an incursion owes that partly to a contract someone bid on. The monopoly is monstrous *and* it is load-bearing, and the people inside it mostly know both things.

### The debt pipeline

A powerful Domain is not a blessing, it is an expensive liability. Active use ruins gear, burns the body, and attracts predators. The Ledger sells the stabilisers, medicine, and weaponry needed to survive that — on credit, at interest. Even its strongest operators are permanently in debt to it.

**This is the engine the campaign runs on.** Every contract taken is a payment against a balance that does not clear. The mechanics of that balance are Burnout and stabiliser doses ([Domains](Domains.md) §4–§5) paid for in bounty bands ([Carrion](Carrion.md) §4).

### Divisions

| Division | Street name | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Contract** | The Apex Cleavers | Licensed crews bankrolled by pharmaceutical, property, and political money. Bids on bounties, fights incursions, runs down Strays who will not register. Stages kills for the cameras while sparing corporate infrastructure. |
| **Salvage** | The Carrion Brokers | Trails combat units in reinforced meat trucks. Carves glands, marrow, and mutated organs before rot sets in; refines them into stabilisers and anti-anomaly munitions. Rarely fights anything healthy. |

**Division friction.** Contract auctions salvage rights and executes any butcher caught harvesting a fallen Cleaver's implants. Salvage skims, bribes dispatchers, and carves off rare glands before the appraisers arrive.

Because the Ledger is a monopoly, a great many divided-loyalty stories happen inside it rather than between factions — one option among several now that every pairing is legal ([Campaign Format](Campaign%20Format.md) §3).

### The Exchange, inside

| Level | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Exchange Floor** (upstairs) | Neon contract boards: bounties, casualty estimates, payout ratios, salvage rates. Brokers haggle territorial rights while dispatchers shout over sirens. |
| **The Lock & Quench** (downstairs) | Part armoury, part black-market clinic. Hunters patch wounds, reload anti-anomaly munitions, and take stabiliser injections against Burnout. |
| **The Cellar** (containment) | Captured Strays and half-dead Carrion in dampening cages — awaiting extraction for parts, or reconditioning into indentured strike teams. |

## 4. The District Marshals — "The Lead-Heads"

The decaying remnants of municipal police and emergency services. Starved of funding because city tax revenue is funnelled into Ledger defence contracts. Ordinary people with no Domains, policing a paranormal city.

**They are the only faction that profits nothing from an incursion.** The Ledger bills for them, the Cult worships them, Strays merely survive them. Marshals get a body count and a repair bill. Their decency is structural — it falls out of their incentives, not their virtue.

- **Evacuation is theirs.** Nobody else gets civilians out. It is unpaid, unglamorous, and the thing they actually exist to do.
- **Losing, not noble.** Underfunded, outgunned, no Domains, no authority over Ledger contractors. Virtue that costs something.
- **Compromised sympathetically.** They look away on Strays because Strays protect the blocks nobody else will. Individual officers still take Salvage bribes; the institution's purpose remains civilian survival.
- **Open to the Domainless.** The only faction a character without a Domain can meaningfully join. The department holds no Domain licence at all, so an officer who does have one is unregistered by definition — a Stray in uniform, and a career's worth of blackmail for anyone who finds out.

**Combat philosophy.** Fire superiority and barricades. Knowing a Domain user can crush steel with a glance, they rely on range, numbers, and military surplus: buckshot, tear gas, road spikes, roadblocks. Every bit of which is **Loud**, which is the Marshals' quiet tragedy — the body doing the evacuating is also filling the clock ([Domains](Domains.md) §2).

**2013 kit.** Battered Crown Victorias with failing transmissions, 12-gauge shotguns with lead slugs, Maglites, VHS dashcams, paper precinct logs, and radios that die the moment they matter.

## 5. The Rupture Cult — "The Hollowed"

An accelerationist network of Domain addicts, failed hunters, and street mystics who hold that Incursions are purges and that human bodies were meant to change.

**Their operational aim is the Ledger.** Stabilisers are leashes. The Ledger sells leashes. Therefore the monopoly must break.

**Take the argument seriously.** The Cult's core claim is true: the people keeping this city alive are permanently indebted to the firm selling them the medicine, and that arrangement was designed. Everything objectionable about the Cult follows from what they decided to *do* about a correct observation. A player who joins them is not playing a villain; they are playing someone who ran out of patience with a system nobody else will name.

**Where they are wrong.** They believe Stigma is what draws the Carrion, and therefore that the Ledger's crews are hypocrites for using Domains at all. The Noise Clock says otherwise: gunfire fills it exactly as fast as a Domain flare ([Domains](Domains.md) §2). A Cult member who works this out has a genuine crisis on their hands, and it is one of the better character arcs available in this setting.

**Methods.** Recruit from Strays on the brink of debt collapse, promising transcendence in exchange for abandoning stabilisers. Hunt Cleavers for their implants. Sabotage containment walls and release caged Spawns. Bait wild Carrion into crowded stations to harvest the panic. The last two are why the other three bodies want them stopped, and no sympathetic reading makes them not worth stopping.

**Combat philosophy.** Suicidal blitzes. They deliberately Overreach to force Burnout, spiking what they can do while their bodies come apart ([Domains](Domains.md) §4).

**2013 kit.** Mini-DV tapes passed hand to hand, hidden boards over Tor on dial-up, pressure-cooker bombs packed with monster gore, amateur tattoo rigs using beast-blood ink.

## 6. Tensions

No pair here is good against evil. Every one of them is two defensible positions that cannot both be acted on.

| Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Ledger ↔ Cult** | Open war, and the campaign's sharpest axis. The Cult exists to break the monopoly; the Ledger refuses them a bounty listing rather than admit the threat is organised. Both are protecting something real. |
| **Marshals ↔ Cult** | The ugliest pair. The Cult treats unpowered humans as an evolutionary dead end and raids precincts to prove it; the Marshals are the one body with nothing to gain from any of it. |
| **Ledger ↔ Marshals** | Contempt and dependency. Corporate immunity passes against municipal rage. Marshals hold the lines contractors abandon, then clean up the blood and eat the repair bill. |
| **Contract ↔ Salvage** | Internal. Exploitation downward, skimming upward. |
| **All → Strays** | Not a faction relationship. Strays are the contested population every faction draws from: recruited, hunted, preyed on, tolerated. |
| **All → Carrion** | The only relationship in the city with no second side to it. |

## 7. Ranks

Low to high. Street names in brackets. Ranks are **fictional standing** (P4, settled): they open doors, assign obligations, and change how people speak to you. They grant no mechanical bonus and cost no PP — rank is something the world confers, which puts it squarely inside the DM's authority over what the world does back ([Campaign Format](Campaign%20Format.md) §2).

| Faction | Ranks |
|---|---|
| **Ledger — Contract** (5) | Junior Associate (Meat Shields) → Chartered Contractor (Line Cleaver) → Lead Asset (The Brand) → Director (Ringmaster) → Board Partner (The Apex) |
| **Ledger — Salvage** (4) | Scrapper (Bucket-Boys) → Knacker (Bone-Saw) → Refiner (Apothecary) → Guild Factor (Carrion Broker) |
| **District Marshals** (6) | Probationary Patrolman (Kevlar Target) → Patrol Officer (Lead-Head) → Detective (Coroner-Cop) → Sergeant (Iron-Shield) → Precinct Captain (Desk-Warden) → Chief of Marshals (Old Brass) |
| **Rupture Cult** (4) | The Tethered (Unmarked) → The Smoldering (Ash-Eaters) → The Resonant (Marrow-Forged) → The Void-Vessel (Apostles) |

Cult ranks are not appointed. They mark how much human physiology the member has shed: still human, first induced Burnout, permanently fused with monster tissue, and finally able to walk into a Rupture without disintegrating. The last of these is adjacent to the Burnout threshold in [Domains](Domains.md) §4 — and a player is the only person who may put their character across it.
