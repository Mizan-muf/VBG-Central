# Port Vane — Setting Guide

**Status:** working draft · **Revised:** 2026-09-24
**Owns:** world facts, the city, incursion process, and terminology.

> Mechanics come from [VBG 2.2](../vbg-2.2/README.md).

## 1. The world

Alternate Earth. The **Fracture** occurred quietly in the late 1990s. By **2013** the world has not ended in fire — it has rotted at the seams.

Nations, trade, and governments still function on paper, but globalisation has fragmented. Land between major cities is abandoned to the **Out-Zones**: unmonitored borderlands nobody patrols.

**2013 is deliberate.** No pervasive surveillance, no drones, no smartphone that resolves a scene. Money buys better gear, not omniscience. Comms fail for reasons inside the fiction, which is what makes independent player scenes workable.

## 2. Port Vane

A sunken port city on a polluted mid-Atlantic estuary, built on tidal mudflats, granite bluffs, and industrial landfill. A fortress-state of concentric rings divided by rusted flood-gates and concrete blast walls. Inner rings sanitised; outer rings left to soak up incursions.

People stay because leaving means the Out-Zones with no walls and no supply lines. Inside there is electricity, cheap canned food, 3G, and black-market stabilisers.

| Ring | Terrain | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — The Spire** | Granite bluffs 200 ft above the flood plain. Glass and steel monoliths, private helipads, fibre trunk lines. Micro-turbines and diesel guarantee unthrottled power. | Ledger leadership at the Old Customs Exchange |
| **2 — The Flats** | Sinking reclaimed marsh. Four-storey brick walk-ups, elevated rail, decommissioned locomotive yards. | Marshals, Strays, Salvage yards |
| **3 — The Sinks** | Low-lying flooded salt marsh, container terminals, petroleum tank farms behind a 30 ft flood-and-blast wall. Streetlights rarely function. Air smells of diesel, sulfur, and brine. | Fighting pits, Cult temples, unlicensed labs |
| **Out-Zones** | Acidic pine barrens, cracked interstates, abandoned 1990s suburbs. No clearing sweeps, no walls. | Feral Shiverer swarms |

**The Black Ditch.** A 150 ft commercial canal cutting through Ring 2. Once coal barges; now an open sewer of industrial sludge and monster runoff.

**Bascule bridges** link Flats neighbourhoods and are the district's chokepoints. During an incursion, handlers bribe bridge-tenders to raise them — trapping civilians and monsters on one side to protect assets on the other.

**Sightlines.** Salt-fog, sodium light, and failed streetlights mean much of Ring 3 runs **Dim** by the VBG light rules, and the Sinks after dark run **Dark** outside a carried lamp. Bring a light and announce yourself, or do not and lose sight past Melee. This is a standing feature of the district, not a special condition.

**The Breakwater.** Miles of shipping containers stacked three high along Ring 3, a makeshift perimeter against Tier II crawling out of the tidal mud.

### Climate

- **Salt-fog and rain.** Coastal fronts trap exhaust and ozone at street level. Sightlines drop under 50 yards.
- **Autumn nor'easters,** September to December, push seawater up the storm drains into Ring 2. Fights happen knee-deep in freezing contaminated water.
- **Static corridors.** Iron-rich bluffs trap and re-reflect Dead Static from frequent nearby Ruptures, leaving pockets that stay unreliable long after the tear that caused them closed. Phones drop, radios loop feedback. **Mechanically:** a static corridor is permanently under Dead Static, and the DM declares it in the opening scene post ([Domains](Domains.md) §2).

## 3. Ruptures and incursions

Where trauma, industrial poison, or heavy Domain use concentrate, physical reality thins. The fabric tears into a **Rupture**: an oily, pitch-black tear in air, asphalt, or sewer wall, emitting a low-frequency hum and toxic fallout.

### Dead Static

One to two hours before a Rupture opens:

- Analog radios scream white noise; CRT screens roll; car engines stall.
- Street animals flee in a frenzy or turn violently on each other.
- The air fills with ozone and sour copper.

When the tear opens, a concussive pulse blows out nearby glass and distorts local gravity for a moment. Then the Carrion come through.

### Anatomy of an incursion

Handled less like a heroic standoff and more like a chemical spill crossed with a riot.

1. **Siren and blast.** Civil defence sirens on telephone poles. EAS tones through radios. Every phone in five miles buzzes: `CODE AMBER — DISTRICT 9. SHELTER IN PLACE. CONTRACTORS EN ROUTE.`
2. **The bidding floor.** The Ledger's bounty ticker updates. Contract crews get first refusal. If the district is poor, the bounty is left to sit until panic drives the council to raise emergency funds.
3. **Street combat.** Crews move in behind lead-shielded barricades. Collateral damage is a tax write-off.
4. **The meat market.** Within minutes of a kill, Salvage trucks roll into the cordon. Technicians strip horns, glands, and marrow before the carcass dissolves or the Marshals cordon the site as evidence.

### What the Carrion come for

Carrion above Tier I hunt active Domain use. A fleeing civilian is food; a Cleaver igniting an ability is irresistible, and they will go through a wall to reach one.

**This costs the Domain user nothing.** It is written on the creature's card as **Stigma draw**, and it means the creature changes target ([Domains](Domains.md) §2). Which cuts both ways: it is how an incursion goes wrong, and it is the only reliable way to pull a Carapace-Render off a crowd. In Port Vane, the person with the fire is the person who can decide to be the one it wants.

**What actually refills a Domain user is a corpse.** Strain does not come back on its own here — only refined Carrion tissue or raw Carrion tissue does ([Domains](Domains.md) §5). That single fact is most of the city: it is why the Ledger holds the Exchange, why Salvage trails every crew, why a Stray eats what they kill, and why nobody walks into the Sinks without counting doses first.

## 4. Lexicon

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **The Fracture** | The event, late 1990s, that first thinned reality and made Domains and Ruptures possible. Nobody agrees what caused it. |
| **Stigma / Domain** | An invasive metaphysical anomaly. Using it strains body and mind, and calls Carrion. |
| **Stray** | Anyone unaffiliated — unregistered, unsponsored, unprotected. A status, not a group. |
| **Chartered Hand / Cleaver** | A licensed Domain user, permitted to kill inside designated containment zones. Within the Ledger, the corresponding rank is Chartered Contractor. |
| **The Sanction** | The licence itself: legal exemption from manslaughter law inside a containment zone, kept current by working — see [Domains](Domains.md) §4. |
| **Carrion / Spawn** | The creatures that come through a Rupture, collectively. Graded in tiers — see [Carrion](Carrion.md). |
| **Incursion** | A Rupture opening and what comes out of it. |
| **Rupture** | The tear itself. |
| **Cairn** | Infrastructure a Spawn has fused with, opening permanent secondary tears. |
| **Dead Static** | Electromagnetic interference preceding or surrounding Carrion activity. A scene condition the DM declares — see [Domains](Domains.md) §2. |
| **Stabiliser** | Refined Carrion tissue, and the only manufactured thing that puts Strain back. One dose restores 2 Strain — see [Domains](Domains.md) §5. |
| **Red stabiliser** | The rare grade. Restores nothing alone; taken with a stabiliser it restores all Strain, and in downtime it removes an Injury. |

## 5. The Bleed-Belt

Officially Interstate Containment Corridor 95. On the street, **the Dead Arteries** — a 120-mile corridor around Port Vane where Carrion outbred containment and long-distance travel collapsed.

**Why travel broke:**

- **Overpass Cairns.** Tier III anchored into flyovers and interchanges, fusing concrete, billboards, and trapped commuter vehicles into breathing chokepoints that drop Tier I onto passing roofs.
- **Aviation blackout.** Ambient Rupture distortion blinds 2013 avionics within five minutes. Gyroscopes fail, altimeters shudder, aircraft go into the marsh.
- **Salt-sludge floods.** Abandoned drainage pumps; tidal rivers breached. Eight-lane stretches under four feet of brackish water, with stripped car frames as underwater barricades.

**How anyone moves:**

| Route | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The Iron Line** | Privately run armoured freight rail. Twin diesel-electrics in three-inch plate with V-shaped "Carrion Catcher" blades and roof gun tubs. A ticket in an unheated cattle car costs six months of factory wages. |
| **Lead-Sled convoys** | Modified 18-wheelers and surplus flatbeds with barred windshields and hydraulic rams. CB radio on quartz crystals, 1998 paper atlases marked in grease pencil. Never stop for breakdowns. |
| **The Low-Road** | On foot along powerline cuts and rail embankments. Attempted only December to February, when sub-zero cold stiffens Tier I. Travellers burn diesel sludge and sulfur to mask scent. |

**Waypoints:** **Toll Plaza Bravo** (Mile 58) — a concrete toll plaza turned fortress, held by rogue ex-cops and deserter Cleavers charging tolls in fuel, canned food, and shells. **The Overpass Cairn** (Mile 14). **The Iron Boneyard** (Mile 32) — four miles of derailed freight hollowed into a Cult underground and ambush staging ground.

**Running it.** Each route is a **Transition Scene** ([Player Rulebook](../vbg-2.2/Player%20Rulebook.md) §4): one to three legs, one obstacle and one roll each, with a Failure turning a leg into an ordinary threat scene. The Iron Line is one leg and expensive; the Low-Road is three and cheap. Rations are required past the first leg, and nobody sets out along the Bleed-Belt without counting their doses first.

> **Later-campaign content.** A separate travel campaign in miniature. Nothing here needs resolving to start play in Port Vane.
