**Owns:** Port Vane bindings, integration constraints, and starter-content requirements.

## 2. Translate the city into recurring play

Start in two connected areas of the Flats, with access to the Black Ditch and competing institutional interests. Leave the Spire, Sinks, and Out-Zones available as context and future expansion. Exact neighbourhood names and local NPCs are new content to draft, not facts already found in the setting.

| Setting feature | Player-led entry point | Persistent consequence to adjudicate |
|---|---|---|
| Flooding and pump failure | Convene residents, recruit repair help, arrange shelter | Safer access, supplies used, new obligations. |
| Bascule bridges | Track access, negotiate passage, investigate who paid a tender | Travel permissions, testimony, faction attention. |
| Salvage yards and corpse decay | Negotiate claims, transport, protection, evidence custody | Registered yield, damaged relationships, contested ownership. |
| Static corridors | Relay messages, escort a witness, establish a contact network | Verified information and communication access. |
| Clinics and stabiliser debt | Organise supplies, bargain over repayment, protect patients | Defined treatment, paid obligations, local access. |
| Institutional friction | Testify, mediate, choose between incompatible promises | Ratified faction response and consequences of loyalty. |

Frequent combat is a central activity of this server. Give these situations recurring, packet-supported monster fights while preserving the people, debts, and consequences that connect them. Characters need one another before, during, and after violence.

## 3. Opening situation: the pump and the bridge

**Proposed scenario, not established canon:** a pump serving a Flats block begins to fail as severe rain approaches. A raised bridge complicates delivery. Salvage wants priority access to an adjacent site; Marshals need a civilian route kept open. A report of Dead Static is unverified.

Seed six opportunities without prescribing a party or solution:

1. Residents call a meeting; PCs can host, challenge, help, or ignore it.
2. A mechanic needs access and replacement parts; several acquisition methods remain possible.
3. A witness offers an account of the bridge closure; the DM owns its truth and further discoveries.
4. A clinic asks for volunteers and space, creating a social/logistics scene.
5. A Salvage representative offers a limited bargain with explicit costs.
6. If evidence supports an incursion, players can pursue warning, evacuation, containment, or combat as separate linked tasks.

Prepare an NPC response sheet, information provenance, the pump project's milestones, and at most three relevant world clocks. Do not predetermine that a Cult conspiracy or a fight must occur. End the opening arc with a public change and unresolved relationships that give players reasons to continue.

## 4. Required adapter work

| Dependency | Proposed treatment | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Domain catalog** | Recover and curate the absent external list, or author an explicitly replacement list. Aim for 20–30 themed entries, starting with a smaller approved pilot selection covering several niches. | Domain-using characters need approved effects, loudness, costs, limits, and examples before their pilot. |
| **Domainless creation** | Develop the explicit optional creation variant in the combat plan; test starting capability, compensation, and later awakening. | Must be resolved before advertising playable Domainless PCs. |
| **P2 — standing** | Retain narrated standing at launch; record deeds and permissions. | Numerical reputation remains unavailable until defined. |
| **P3 — conflicting memberships** | Present the conflict and allow a choice; adjudicate each faction response separately. | Write exposure and consequence guidance before concealed loyalties drive high-stakes scenes. |
| **P4 — ranks** | Keep fictional rank and mechanical advancement separate. | Define costs/prerequisites before a rank grants mechanical power. |
| **P5 — bounty economy** | Implement the small catalog and payout-band proposal in World Systems; include first Armor access. | D2/Q4 and the supply/repayment budget must be settled locally first. |
| **Burnout and stabilisers** | Test the World Systems proposal against the intended dependence on medicine. | Show recurring demand in an ordinary Domain career, attainable individual debt relief, and no charges for real-world absence. A drug cannot mechanically prevent an undefined condition. |
| **P6 — Tier IV** | Defer Cold Silents and hidden metaplot; use discoverable local mysteries. | Later content needs a named staff owner and clues, not permanent dependence on one GM. |
| **P7 — membership variety** | Use Contract/Salvage disputes, professional networks, crews, and personal loyalties. | Add another faction only through an explicit setting revision. |
| **Cult PCs** | Recommend staff-run antagonists for the pilot; playable membership is a later decision. | This restricts a possible character option and needs explicit adoption; it is not a settled setting prohibition. |

The absent P1 Google Doc has not been accessed or recreated here. The plan can proceed without it; adapting its actual entries requires its contents. A pilot-created list must be labelled new material, not presented as the missing source.

For the stabiliser/debt gate, simulate at least four fictional work-and-recovery periods for ordinary Domain users, frequent overchannel users, and Domainless PCs. Account for every dose, income source, repayment, and treatment. Pass only if ordinary Domain careers still generate the setting's demand, a player can achieve recorded relief, and absence creates no costs. If overchannel-only burnout fails, revise that model or explicitly revise the setting dependency; do not call the adapter ready based on combat balance alone.

## 5. Combat and content binding

- Build reusable local defence, containment, salvage-protection, pursuit, and rescue packets for authorised runners. Endorsed players can run and participate in public deterministic versions without a staff DM present; their authority ends at the packet boundary.
- Maps use named footholds, exact routes, and independent firing lanes. Port Vane's bridge controls, trucks, stairs, flooded landings, shutters, and barricades should change routes and angles through printed actions, not improvised distance judgments.
- Use Carrion instincts to give creatures objectives: feed, pursue active Domains, anchor into infrastructure. Avoid identical enemies distinguished only by Resistance.
- Keep Tier I–III stat mappings provisional until the tactical damage and encounter model has been tested. A large event can include multiple local objectives without putting 20 PCs in one initiative sequence.
- Give Domainless characters jobs with decisive effects: sever a feed line, suppress a route, coordinate evacuation, operate a barrier, carry out treatment, or expose a vulnerability.
- “Loud hits harder” is current setting fiction, not a settled numerical damage bonus. Approved effect cards must define how destructive power, cost, attention, and counterplay fit together.
- Link loudness, burnout, and economic consequences to their [World Systems owner](World%20Systems%20Plan.md); do not maintain parallel clocks or charge the same event twice.
- Keep Bleed-Belt travel and Tier III city sieges for later releases. The initial campaign already has enough friction inside the city to sustain varied play.
