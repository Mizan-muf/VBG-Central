# Very Basic RPG 2.2 — GM Guide

**Status:** working draft · **Revised:** 2026-09-24  
**Owns:** GM procedures for presenting scenes, approving Domains, adjudicating actions, and resolving asynchronous posts. It does not override shared mechanics.

> A working draft does not replace a published rule until approved. Anything marked pending is not playable; see the [Decision Log](Decision%20Log.md).

Use this guide alongside the [Player Rulebook](Player%20Rulebook.md), which owns every shared mechanic referenced here.

## 1. Run a scene

State what is happening, what is at risk, the visible threats, known Target Numbers, relevant distance bands, and what will happen if a threat goes unanswered. Give players enough concrete information to declare a response.

Every scene post also states, openly:

- **The area's Noise Clock**, as a number out of 6.
- **Each creature's awareness** — Unaware, Searching, or Aware.
- **Light** — Lit, Dim, or Dark — wherever it is not obviously Lit.
- **Available cover**, and who is **engaged** with what.

None of those four is hidden information. A player cannot plan around a clock they cannot see, and withholding it converts a tactical layer into a guessing game.

For each cycle:

1. Post the scene, threats, stakes, known TNs, clock, and awareness.
2. Receive player posts for up to 24 hours.
3. Resolve all actions together against the established fiction.
4. Update positions, resources, hazards, conditions, creature Resistance, the clock, awareness, and emerging threats.
5. Start the next cycle.

The 24-hour player window is a real-world posting limit. It does not set in-fiction duration, recovery time, sustained-effect duration, or a Downed character's treatment deadline.

**A scene ends** when its stakes resolve and no threat presses the group. That boundary is what "once per scene" means, so state it plainly rather than letting a scene drift.

## 2. Adjudicate declared actions

Ask for a clear objective, method, and stakes. A roll is required when the outcome is uncertain or the action forces an outcome on another creature. Do not add a roll merely because an approved ability effect is produced without uncertainty.

Use TN 10 for routine pressure, 15 for standard risk including combat, 20 for desperate or precise danger, and 25+ for extreme circumstances. A player proposing a TN when acting independently must name the difficulty before rolling. The Player Rulebook §2 owns the veto standard: **reject a proposed TN that does not fit the fiction, whether it is too low or too high.** The same standard covers a Trait the player claims is relevant when it is not — the player asserts relevance, and you reject it only when the fiction does not support it.

Resolve the stated objective through the Player Rulebook outcome table. Treat Success with Cost as success. Let the player propose a fitting cost, then set or revise it when hidden information, severity, or fiction requires it. A cost never kills, never touches another player's character or property without that player's agreement, and never exceeds what the action stood to gain. A Critical Success never adds damage, never grants a second action, and never exceeds what was declared.

On Failure, use the player's proposed consequence when it fits; otherwise state one that follows from the scene. Do not add an extra reaction roll after failure.

One roll can resolve an action and a threat only if the action meaningfully addresses that threat. For example, an attempt to shove away an attacker can prevent their strike; searching a desk while attacked cannot. A threat never gets its own defensive roll: it is addressed by the Major Action, answered by a conditional response, or it applies. See Player Rulebook §4.

Resolve each cycle in the order the Player Rulebook §4 sets out — movement, then actions and threats together, then conditional responses, then consequences. Post order is not initiative and settles nothing but genuinely incompatible declarations. Every hit declared in a cycle lands even if the creature was already going to fall; the excess is wasted. A character Downed during a cycle still resolves the action they declared.

A post that leaves out a part simply forfeits it. Do not query an incomplete post, and do not fill the gap with the missed-window default; that default is for silence.

## 3. Approve Ability Domains

A Domain is a **sphere of effect**, described in one sentence, that the character can produce by spending Strain. You approve it at character creation and again when a player buys a second or third with PP.

Approve a Domain when all four of these hold:

1. **It names a medium, not a goal.** "Flame", "the weight of things", "what metal remembers" are spheres. "Winning fights", "being right", "getting out of trouble" are not.
2. **A player can predict what it cannot do.** If neither of you can name three things outside its scope, it is too wide.
3. **It fits the three scales.** There is an obvious 0-Strain version, an obvious 1-Strain version, and an obvious 2-Strain version. A Domain with no small uses becomes a nuke the player fires once a scene.
4. **It leaves the rest of the table something to do.** Not because it is weak, but because it is specific.

| Verdict | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Accepted** | *Flame — shapes and directs open fire that already burns, or that I kindle by hand.* | Names a medium, has an obvious floor and ceiling, and has a clear limit: no fire, no ability. |
| **Accepted** | *Rot — accelerates decay in dead organic matter I can touch.* | Narrow, strange, and useless against half the problems in a scene, which is a feature. |
| **Accepted** | *Hush — dampens sound in a space I can see.* | Small on paper, enormous with a Noise Clock running. Scope is unambiguous. |
| **Rejected** | *Telekinesis.* | Too wide. It has no medium, no limits, and quietly replaces every other Domain at the table. |
| **Rejected** | *Luck.* | Names a goal, not a sphere. Every use is an argument about outcomes. |
| **Rejected** | *Healing others' wounds instantly and at any range.* | Range and instancy must be approved explicitly; "miraculous or unlimited" is the one thing Domains may never be. |

**When you reject, counter-offer.** *Telekinesis* becomes *the weight of things — I make objects heavier or lighter within sight*. That keeps the player's idea and gives the table a scope everyone can reason about.

**Reach.** Default reach is Close / Room. A Domain that works at Far or Distant needs that stated in the approval, and you should charge for it in scope: a long-reach Domain should be narrower in what it does.

## 4. Present threats

Creatures do not roll attack dice. Announce the threat, its reach, the pressure it creates, a suggested response and TN when appropriate, and its unresolved consequence. Players may propose other fitting responses.

Use the creature's stated behavior when resolving hazards: it can stop, take another route, or push through. It does not roll to decide. A hazard placed to catch a creature needs a roll; a hazard established in advance does not guarantee entry.

Keep consequences concrete. State damage, displacement, loss, injury, constraint, or escalating danger. If a creature action has a special condition, reveal discoverable clues through the fiction.

### Running the clock

The Noise Clock is the pressure that makes quiet play worth choosing. Run it honestly:

- **Tick it when the fiction ticks it**, using the item and action ratings the Player Rulebook §4 and Item Glossary §1 supply. A firearm is Loud whoever fires it.
- **Decay it** by 1 after a cycle in which nothing above Silent happened, and by 1 when the group leaves for a new area without pursuit. Do not let players farm decay by walking in and out of a doorway.
- **At 4–5**, something searches the area and moving unseen costs +2 TN. Show the search. It is a warning, and a warning that is not visible is a trap.
- **At 6**, the thing arrives. Decide what that is *before* the clock reaches it, not after — then reset the clock to 1.

A module may add its own noise triggers. It never adds a second clock.

## 5. Movement and escape

Track fictional position through the Player Rulebook distance bands. Require every movement declaration to identify origin and destination, then apply terrain, obstacles, pursuit, encumbrance, and conditions.

Ordinary accompanying movement reaches up to Close / Room along the actual route, and **cannot leave the band while the character is engaged**. Walking away from something pressing you is not movement; it is an Escape.

Escape requires a Major Action dedicated to getting away, at **TN 10 unengaged, 15 against one threat, 20 against several or a cut-off route**. Success puts one band between the character and everything that was engaging them; a second consecutive success takes them out of the scene. It is not a movement multiplier and grants no extra distance beyond that band.

**Pursuit:** a pursuer closes one band per cycle unless held off, and gives up after two consecutive cycles at greater distance. Say which it is each cycle, so a fleeing player knows whether fleeing is working.

**Transition Scene:** use the procedure in Player Rulebook §4 for long travel — one to three legs, one obstacle and one roll each, with a Failure converting a leg into an ordinary threat scene. Do not silently convert travel into unlimited action movement, and do not award PP for arriving somewhere.

## 6. Downed characters, injury, and death

When a character reaches 0 Strain, do three things in the same post: say they are Downed, name the **Injury** the harm caused and its practical effect, and announce an in-fiction treatment deadline tied to that injury and the danger around them. Do not apply a fixed real-world timer, and do not let the 24-hour posting window stand in for the deadline.

Being Downed is how this game hurts people. It is not how it kills them.

- **If the deadline passes untreated,** the Injury becomes permanent until properly treated. The character does not die of neglect.
- **Further hits on a Downed character** worsen the Injury. They do not kill.
- **Stabilization** is a Major Action within Melee reach: automatic with medical supplies, TN 10 without. A stabilized character is conscious at 0 Strain and can speak, take Free Actions, and crawl within their band.
- **Strain Transfer** can reach a Downed character too: the first 1 Strain transferred ends the Downed state. It does not remove the Injury.
- **Death** requires one of exactly two things, both of which you must put in front of the players *before* they resolve. Either a stated killing — a creature card whose action says it kills, or an attacker who declares a killing blow on a helpless character — or a danger you announced as lethal while it could still be avoided. Drowning, a long fall, a fire with no exit. Announce it in the post that presents it.

Never convert an ordinary blow into a death after the fact. If a scene is meant to be able to kill, say so while the players can still decide what to do about it. **No player character dies at another player character's hands without that player's agreement.**

A Downed character whose player misses the posting window does nothing at all; the take-cover default cannot apply to them. Their deadline keeps running, which is the other players' problem to solve.

## 7. Build and run creatures

Use the [Creature Blueprint](Creature%20Blueprint.md) to construct creature cards. Before the encounter, set Resistance, **Senses**, starting awareness, the noise the creature makes, 3–6 visible or discoverable action options, behavior, weaknesses, and any special defeat condition.

Resistance is the only defeat track. Remove a box for each damage after applying creature-specific protections. At zero boxes, resolve the declared defeat unless the creature's special condition says what happens first. A condition must explain its trigger, effect, duration when relevant, and discoverable clue.

**Resistance is duration, not difficulty.** If you want a harder fight, write a harder condition, a nastier threat, or better ground — not a longer Resistance track. A card with six boxes and nothing else has made the fight slower, not more dangerous.

## 8. Award Progression Points

Award PP per session or completed arc, to every player equally, using the table in Player Rulebook §8. A typical session is 2–5 PP.

- **Never award per cycle.** It turns posting frequency into advancement.
- **Never award for damage dealt.** Nothing in VBG rewards volume of violence, and a table that avoided a fight earns the same as one that won it.
- **Never award PP to one player as a prize.** Uneven PP is how a shared campaign quietly becomes one person's story.
- **Require a fictional reason for a purchase**, and require that it appeared in play. Do not require anything beyond that; PP is not a negotiation.

PP buys the six upgrades in Player Rulebook §8 and nothing else. It is not currency: it does not buy items, information, favours, standing, or fictional outcomes.

## 9. Maintain rule authority

- Link to the Player Rulebook rather than copying shared rules into creature cards or item entries.
- Put creature construction changes in the Creature Blueprint.
- Put item numbers, stacks, kits, bands, and material quantities in the Item Glossary.
- Put unresolved questions and temporary adjudications in the Decision Log.
- Do not treat a pending document entry as a playable rule.
- A setting module scaffolds on these rules: it adds triggers, catalogs, fiction, and gear entries in its own format. It does not restate a mechanic, and where it appears to, this set wins.
